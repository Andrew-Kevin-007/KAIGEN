# KAIGEN v3 stretch items as built: the policy-key split and `sweep_to_safe`

Status: 2026-11-04. Code: `contracts/mamori/src/lib.rs`. Tests: `src/tests/sweep.rs`, `src/tests/policy.rs`, and the extended `src/tests/loss_bound.rs`, `admin_surface.rs`, `attest_check.rs`. Unit-test VM until 2026-11-07, when the owner ran `sweep_to_safe` and the policy path on a testnet drill vault with `stretchdrill` (10 s delay) [TESTNET-REHEARSAL.md, third run]. No sandbox run, no mainnet. No audit. Nothing is committed. Sources of the design: ROADMAP.md (stretch row, Q2, Q5), ARCHITECTURE.md (2.2, 2.8, 5, 6), THREAT-MODEL.md, LOSS-BOUND.md, ATTEST-CHECK.md. Where the owner decided a question (the safe account), the decision is applied as given; where the docs left a choice open, the most conservative reading was taken and is listed below with the reason and the attack it prevents.

## 1. What exists

| Export | Gate | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `set_policy_account(account \| null)` | admin | Names the one account whose `propose_*` calls are accepted, or switches the policy path off. Drops every waiting proposal in the same call. |
| `propose_add_daily_key(key, allowance)`, `propose_set_token_limit(token, limit)`, `propose_set_pending_limit(token, limit)`, `propose_allow_recipient(account)` | the policy account | Each records a proposal and returns its id. Nothing changes yet. |
| `apply(id)` | anyone | Runs a proposal once the withdrawal delay has passed and before it lapses, through the same private function the admin's own method uses. |
| `cancel_proposal(id)` | admin | Throws one proposal away, before or after its delay. |
| `set_safe_account(account)`, `cancel_safe_account_change()` | admin | The safe account changes only after the withdrawal delay; the change can be cancelled until then. |
| `add_watcher(account)`, `remove_watcher(account)`, `set_emergency(secs)` | admin | Up to 4 watcher accounts; an emergency window of at most 7 days in which they may sweep (0 closes it). |
| `sweep_to_safe(id)` | the admin, or a watcher inside an emergency window | Moves one live queued request out of the queue and its amount to the safe account. |
| `on_sweep_done(...)` | `#[private]` | Logs `sweep_failed` if the transfer failed. Puts nothing back. |
| views `policy_account`, `safe_account`, `watchers`, `emergency_until`, `proposals` | open | The settings. `attest()` carries the same five (ATTEST-CHECK.md 2.1). |

Unchanged on purpose: `request_withdraw` (signature, return type, every refusal still a panic and uncounted), the 24 h window and its counting rule, the delay floor, all existing admin methods (they keep working as before: the cold admin key can still do everything it could), `check`, the events already in the table.

## 2. Design choices

Each row: the choice, the reason, the attack it prevents, and what was eliminated. "Admin" means a call whose predecessor is the vault, as everywhere else in Mamori.

| # | Choice | Reason and the attack it prevents | Eliminated |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **The policy actor and the watchers are accounts, identified by the predecessor of the call. They are not function-call keys on the vault.** This departs from the docs sketch (ARCHITECTURE.md section 6 says "classical function-call policy key" and "watchers are classical keys using `signer_account_pk`"). | The docs' own warning is that `assert_admin` is `predecessor == self` and every key of the vault satisfies it, so the method list of each such key is the only thing between it and the admin methods, and cage-check has to police it. An account's predecessor cannot be forged and a key of the vault can never carry another account's name, so there is no method list to get wrong: a key planted on the vault with `propose_*` or `sweep_to_safe` in its list is simply refused (predecessor is the vault, not the policy account). It also means no new function-call key class on the vault (the key list cage-check reads is unchanged by this feature), no new gas allowance outside the cap, and **no read of `signer_account_pk`**: the closed list of signer-key readers in `admin_surface.rs` (`assert_daily_key`, `send_small`, `request_withdraw`) is unchanged, which matters because what the host returns for an ML-DSA signer is unverified (ARCHITECTURE.md 2.2). | Key-based policy key and watchers: need a signer read in methods the ML-DSA admin can reach (the admin could not call `sweep_to_safe` at all if that read aborts), and a method-list rule in cage-check for each. Residual: the vault cannot see how well the policy account's or a watcher's own keys are held. See section 4. |
| D2 | The policy account, every watcher and the safe account can **never be the vault itself** (refused with a fixed text). | With the vault named, every key on the vault (a planted one included) would be a policy actor or a watcher, which is exactly the hole D1 closes. A safe account equal to the vault would make a sweep move nothing anywhere and look like protection. | Allowing it and trusting cage-check. |
| D3 | The policy account can propose only a **closed list of four loosenings**: add a daily key, set a NEP-141 cap, set a pending limit, allow a recipient. Not proposable: the NEAR cap (no setter exists at all), intents keys, the allowlist switch, the minimum send, the strike limit, and everything about the policy account, safe account, watchers and emergency window. | The list is the settings an owner changes in daily life and that move the bound (C, P) or the keys. Intents keys sit outside the cap altogether (LOSS-BOUND A2), and the authority settings decide who can act: a policy account that could name its own successor, a watcher or the safe account would rewrite the split from inside. The other settings are tightening or not worth a delayed path. | A generic "call any admin method later". |
| D4 | The delay of a proposal, and of a safe-account change, is **`delay_secs`**, the withdrawal delay. No new parameter, no change to `init`. | "No shorter than the withdrawal delay" (the owner's rule for the safe account, the docs' Safe-timelock precedent for proposals): loosening is never faster than a queued withdrawal could leave, and the admin's look cadence assumed by the bound (A4) is the same period. A production `init` refuses a delay under 24 h, so the floor carries over to both for free; a drill build can have a shorter delay and is already red under ATTEST-2. An `init` parameter would also change the ABI every deploy script calls. | A separate policy delay (a second number to misconfigure). |
| D5 | A proposal **lapses 24 h after it can first run**; at most **8** wait at once; the next proposal clears the lapsed ones first (and logs it). | Same reasoning as `EXECUTE_WINDOW_SECS` for queued requests: an admin who looked and let something through should not find it live weeks later. The cap keeps `proposals()`, `attest()` and the clearing bounded, and a stolen policy account cannot fill storage. | Proposals that live until applied or cancelled. |
| D6 | `apply` is **permissionless**, runs the change first and removes the proposal only when the change did not refuse. | A keeper needs no authority and the timing decides, not who calls. A refusal (for example a key still awaiting deletion) leaves the proposal for the next try. Doing the change first also keeps the table consistent in any caller's view; on the chain a panic discards every write anyway. | Policy-only apply. |
| D7 | A proposal runs through **the same private function** as the admin's own method (`record_daily_key`, `put_pending_limit`, `put_allowed`, the token-limit insert). The one place it is narrower: **a proposed key's gas allowance may not exceed the default (0.25 NEAR)**. | One code path means a proposal cannot do what the admin could not, and every refusal of the admin call (revoked key, too many keys, kaigen lockdown, allowlist full) is a refusal of `apply`. An allowance is gas the key can burn from the vault's balance outside every cap (LOSS-BOUND A3, "up to 8 keys x 0.25 NEAR"): the cap means a stolen policy account that nobody cancels in time gets a key worth at most the default. | Letting the policy account give any allowance. |
| D8 | **Replacing or clearing the policy account drops every waiting proposal, in the same call**, and logs them. | Rotating a stolen policy account must also take back what it asked for, atomically. Cancelling eight proposals one by one races `apply` for the whole delay. | Cancel one by one only. |
| D9 | **The safe account is named by the admin only**, takes effect only after the withdrawal delay (the first one too), a second call before then replaces the waiting one and restarts the clock, and the admin can cancel it until it takes effect. | The destination of every sweep is the one setting that turns a watcher's power into somebody's loss. No daily key and no watcher can reach it; a typo or a rushed change by the admin has a full delay to be noticed and cancelled; the old one serves until the last nanosecond. The first one waits too so there is one rule and no window with no safe account being quietly replaced. | Setting it at `init` (the docs' sketch; the owner decided against it); an immediate first set. |
| D10 | **`sweep_to_safe(id)` has no destination argument.** Token and amount are those of the request; the request's own payee is ignored and gets nothing. | The only place value can go is the safe account in force at that block. A watcher cannot send anywhere else, and cannot move more than a request holds. | A `to` argument checked against a list. |
| D11 | A sweep **takes one live request out of the queue and touches nothing else**: no cap, no 24 h window, no request budget (none is refunded), no strike, no key, no setting. It is refused for an expired request and while no safe account is in force. | "Only reduces exposure": it can only take an entry out of the queue, so what a daily key could still send can only go down (LOSS-BOUND.md 9). No budget refund keeps the proof of the bound (the 24 h request budget) monotone, and means sweeping cannot be used to make room. An expired request can never run, so moving its value protects nothing; refusing it also keeps a stolen watcher from moving value that was not at risk. | Sweeping the vault's balance (far more than the docs' per-request sweep); sweeping expired requests. |
| D12 | **A sweep whose transfer fails is not put back.** The value stays in the vault, the request is gone (like `cancel`), and `sweep_failed` is logged. | A put-back of an item that left the queue is exactly the path that once carried 1.5 pending limits out of one window (`on_execute_done`, `requests_before`, LOSS-BOUND.md 2.2). Not putting back cannot add to any bound. Cost: a safe account that is not registered with a NEP-141 token makes that token's sweeps fail and lose the request (not the value); register the safe account with each listed token. | Re-queueing on failure. |
| D13 | **Who may sweep**: the admin always (no window needed); a watcher only while an **admin-declared emergency window** is open (`now < emergency_until`), at most **7 days** per declaration, **4 watchers** at most, all managed by the admin only. | A stolen watcher is idle outside a window, so it cannot censor honest withdrawals in normal operation, and inside one the worst it does is grief (value only ever goes to the safe account). The 7-day cap makes an owner who leaves for longer declare again instead of leaving watchers armed for ever. Watchers cannot add watchers, open windows or name the safe account. | Always-armed watchers; windows with no end. |
| D14 | Sweeps are **not paused by the era**. | A warning freezes the queue (`execute` refuses) and that is exactly when an owner wants it out of the vault's reach. At kaigen `lock_down` has already discarded the queue. | Gating on a calm era. |
| D15 | **State layout: seven fields appended** after `requested_by_key`; the v3-core bytes are an unchanged prefix. | Listed in section 5. The pin test now holds both the whole layout and the old hex as a prefix, so a later edit cannot reorder the core while only updating the long string. | Reordering or reusing a field. |
| D16 | `attest()` gains five fields, `attest_version` stays 1 ("adding a field does not raise it", ATTEST-CHECK.md 2.1). | The docs call for cage-check to see these settings. They are informational like the rest of `attest()`: a full-access key can deploy code that lies, so cage-check reads the code hash and keys from RPC. | A new attest version. |

## 3. The attack each part is for

| Attacker | Capability | What stops or bounds it |
|---|---|---|
| Stolen policy account, admin looks within the delay | Can propose; nothing else | Every proposal waits `delay_secs`; the admin sees it in `proposals()` and the events, cancels it, or rotates the account (`set_policy_account`), which drops all of them at once. |
| Stolen policy account, admin does not look | Can raise a cap or a pending limit, or add a key with at most 0.25 NEAR of gas, or add a payee, after the delay and within 24 h | This is the whole point of the split and is bounded by the admin's look cadence (LOSS-BOUND A4, now A11). It is not stopped. The loss bound simply uses the raised limits from the moment `apply` runs. |
| Stolen watcher, no window | Nothing | Refused: "no emergency". |
| Stolen watcher, window open | Can sweep any live queued request, to the safe account only | A censorship grief (honest withdrawals must be asked for again). No loss unless the safe account is also compromised. Remove the watcher or close the window. |
| Stolen watcher and a compromised safe account | Moves queued requests to the attacker | Bounded: what leaves the queue in a window is at most 2 pending limits, and everything that leaves the vault at most the cap + 2 pending limits (LOSS-BOUND.md 9). The safe account is the owner's own account and outside the threat model; this row says what its loss would add. |
| Planted function-call key on the vault with `propose_*` or `sweep_to_safe` listed | Reaches the method with predecessor = the vault | Refused by `assert_policy` ("policy account only") and, for `sweep_to_safe`, treated as the admin: **that case is the existing hole** (any key reaching an admin method is the admin, ARCHITECTURE.md 2.2), caught by cage-check, not by this feature. |
| A daily key | `send_small`, `request_withdraw` only (runtime) | Cannot reach any new method: the runtime restricts its method list to those two (the access-key layer, which the unit VM does not have; shown on a sandbox for the existing methods). The new accounts, for their part, are refused by every admin and daily method (`the_policy_account_a_watcher_and_the_safe_account_can_call_no_admin_method_and_no_daily_method`). |
| The admin makes a typo in the safe account | Wrong destination | A full delay to notice and `cancel_safe_account_change`. |
| Policy account fills the proposal table | Storage | At most 8 entries of bounded size; lapsed ones cleared by the next proposal; `set_policy_account` clears all. |

## 4. Unverified and residual

* **Testnet run, 2026-11-07 (drill vault, 10 s delay).** `sweep_to_safe` moved exactly the request's amount (0.0100 NEAR) to the safe account, and the refusals held. `apply` before the delay was refused, and `apply` after it ran, so `PromiseOrValue<()>` ran on the real runtime. Not run on a chain: the watcher path, a NEP-141 sweep, three of the four `propose_*` methods, and a separate policy account with its own key. No per-method gas figure is in the sources; the vault's total fall for one sweep was 0.010704 NEAR, the amount plus 0.000704 NEAR gas. `cargo check --target wasm32-unknown-unknown` passes. [TESTNET-REHEARSAL.md, third run]
* The policy account and the watchers are accounts whose keys the vault cannot see. How they are held (a hardware wallet, a multisig, a hot server) is the owner's decision and sets the real strength of the split.
* cage-check (`jobs/`) was not touched. The rows worth adding are in ATTEST-CHECK.md 2.3. With this design the key list it reads does not change, so the existing checks and the planted-key attack (8) are unaffected.
* A swept NEP-141 request whose safe account is not registered with the token fails and is not put back (D12).
* The first safe account takes a full delay to come into force; a vault that has just been deployed cannot sweep for that long. The watchers have nothing to sweep to before then.
* The admin's cold key remains full-access and can still bypass any timelock (ARCHITECTURE.md 6). The split makes a cold key you almost never use possible; it does not make the contract timelock a security control against the cold key.
* `apply` runs a change even if the policy account that proposed it has become suspect since, unless the admin rotated it (which drops the proposals) or cancelled the proposal.

## 5. State and ABI changes (all listed, the vault will be redeployed)

State fields appended to `Mamori`, in this order, after `requested_by_key`: `policy_account: Option<AccountId>`, `proposals: IterableMap<u64, Proposal>` (prefix `o`), `next_proposal: u64`, `safe_account: Option<AccountId>`, `safe_pending: Option<PendingSafe>`, `watchers: IterableSet<AccountId>` (prefix `t`), `emergency_until: u64`. A deployed v3-core vault cannot read this state without a `migrate`; `init` is unchanged.

ABI: +7 admin exports, +4 policy exports, +1 `apply`, +1 `sweep_to_safe`, +1 private callback `on_sweep_done`, +5 views, `attest()` +5 fields. No existing signature, return type or refusal text changed. New events (ATTEST-CHECK.md section 5): `set_policy_account`, `propose`, `proposal_expired`, `proposal_cancelled`, `proposal_applied`, `set_safe_account`, `safe_account_change_cancelled`, `add_watcher`, `remove_watcher`, `set_emergency`, `sweep_to_safe`, `sweep_failed`. Every refusal of a new method is a panic: none counts a strike, and none changes the strike rules.

## 7. Sandbox attacks that could be affected (ATTACK-RESULTS.md, `tests-integration`, `jobs/`)

Not run here (out of scope, no chain). None of attacks 1 to 9 calls a new method, and every existing method keeps its signature, return type and refusal texts. What does change:

* The wasm is a new build with a new state layout: every attack that deploys it needs the new wasm, and a vault deployed from the v3-core wasm cannot be upgraded onto it without a `migrate` (redeploy, as planned).
* Attack 8 and `cage_check.rs` read `attest()`. `jobs/src/cage.mjs` (`parseAttestation`, read-only here) picks the fields it needs by name and ignores the five new ones; `attest_version` stays 1. Its key rules are untouched because the policy account and the watchers are not keys of the vault.
* Attacks that execute on a chain (2, 6, the timed ones) go through `execute`, which this work did not change. Their gas figures do not move.
* The release wasm is 56.7 KB larger: the global-contract cost estimate (G2) and `REPRODUCIBLE-BUILD.md` hashes need the new build.
* New sandbox attacks worth writing, none exists: a watcher that sweeps outside a window; a policy account that proposes and is cancelled; a sweep to a safe account that has no NEP-141 registration (the transfer should fail and `sweep_failed` should log).
